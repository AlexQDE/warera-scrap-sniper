## 2. Phase 1 — fix and simplify

**Compact annotations** (`extension/lib/equipment.mjs`, `content.css`): one line per offer — tag, scrap profit + ROI, resale estimate + sample, a real `Details` button (`aria-expanded`, keyboard, focus preserved across re-renders, row click not triggered). Details: dismantle walk, price vs scrap floor, resale evidence (sample, window, capping, low–high, quartiles, MAD spread, last fill, pace), stat rank among listed peers, quote age. States: `READING`, `NO QUOTE` with the reason, `STALE` with the last quote kept, and for resale `select this item` / `reading sales` / `sales read failed` / `N of 5 fills` / `no comparable fills`.

**Resale thresholds** (`extension/lib/resale.mjs`): `MIN_RESALE_SAMPLE = 5` supports the median and range; `MIN_PERCENTILE_PEERS = 8` gates percentile ranks and quartile scenarios. Tests: `resale.test.mjs` (4 → insufficient, 5 → estimate without quartiles, 7 → still none, 8 → quartiles; rank refused at 7 peers, granted at 8; ties; comparables by code and ±10 durability), `annotations.test.mjs` (the same boundaries through the rendered row).

**Readable-stat count** (`extension/lib/offers.mjs`): `readStats`, `summarizeOffers` and `offerDimensions` keep stat readability, quote availability and freshness apart from the sales read. Test `annotations.test.mjs › keeps the readable-stat count when the sales read fails` renders a sales error and asserts the count and the SNIPE verdict are unchanged while the resale column reports the failure.

**Settings page** (`extension/popup.html`, `popup.js`): no `min-width`; common settings first (key, decisions, modules), the rest under `<details>` Advanced; verified at 320 / 380 / 560 px (screenshots below).

**Loader** (`extension/lib/dom.mjs`, `equipment.mjs`): what 1.4.1 actually did was not a 100 ms interval but a 100 ms coalescing scheduler that, on every native mutation burst, re-read every BUY button's `innerText`, every row's `innerText`, every frame's computed border, and swept every `div` for the tax notice, document-wide. Now: scans are scoped to the list container found last time (full scan every 30 s or when it is gone), row text and frame colour are cached per row and re-read only when the row's native text changes or the cache epoch turns, the tax notice and grid frames are reused while connected, BUY detection uses `textContent`, and annotation HTML is rebuilt only when its memo key changes. The 100 ms scheduler, cancellation on dispose, freshness checks, the five-page sales cap and all cleanup paths are unchanged (`lifecycle.test.mjs`).

Measured on the same fixture (`fixtures/market.html?item=jet&rows=60`, 40 bursts of 3 appended nodes, 150 ms apart), counters installed before the app starts:

## 3. Phase 2 — Craft Desk

Pure maths in `craft.mjs` (25 tests): tick rounding that keeps exact ticks exact under float noise, recipes that are never guessed, cost per craft/batch with unknown totals when a used input has no price, buying now (walked asks, no price past observed depth) against placing bids (join or front of queue, conditional saving), tax applied once (deducted or added), EV over a validated distribution (`invalid` when probabilities do not sum to one, `unavailable` with the covered share when an outcome has no value; a 10 % chance at 100 g and 90 % at 1 g is 10.9 g, not 100 g), break-even and target-ROI ceilings with reasons, ranking with EV-less plans last.

Adapter in `craftdata.mjs` (9 tests): recipes from settings/manual inputs/fixtures (validated per code), prices from the scrap book and the steel book with freshness or from manual input, tax from manual → saved → page notice → none, outcomes from comparable fills (one sales-weighted outcome at the median, labelled) or a manual bucket table; a synthetic fixture that says it is synthetic.

Panel in `craftdesk.mjs` (9 jsdom tests): 6 × 6 ROI matrix by tier and slot, editable prices/batch/tax/target with sources, recipe entry per cell saved to settings, cost, buy-now vs bids, EV with probability of beating the cost, ceilings, listing guidance (balanced at 5 fills; quick/patient at 8) with evidence (sample, window, capping, spread, last fill, pace, read time), and the ledger.

What the desk cannot do here and says so: no recipe is shipped (cells read `no recipe` until entered), no outcome distribution by roll (the fills read by 1.4.1's API path carry `state` but no stat), steel depth comes from the resource books already read for the case strip (20 levels a side).

## 4. Phase 3 — craft ledger

`ledger.mjs` (10 tests) + worker messages `ledgerGet`/`ledgerSet` (`controller.mjs`, tested): manual entries with cost basis from inferred (quotes of the moment) or manual prices, result attributes, list → sold with proceeds / kept / scrapped / unlisted transitions, realized profit only on recorded sales with a known basis, open pieces estimated and labelled, JSON export, import merged by id with the newer copy winning and malformed entries counted. Stored under `craftLedger`, bounded to 500 entries, kept across key changes and cache cleanup. Writes are operation-based and revisioned: a tab sends only the entries it changed and the ids it removed, the worker merges them onto what is stored, deletions are remembered as tombstones, and an open desk picks up another tab's write within a tick (Codex review findings on the first push and on the fix; its re-review of the fix asked for the operation-based writes and the memo ageing, a third pass for the row memo to carry the estimate itself, a fourth for recipe saves to travel as per-code operations confirmed before the inputs clear, and a fifth for tombstones to carry the revision of their deletion, so a stale tab's later-stamped edit of a deleted entry no longer revives it and the desk says the entry was deleted in another tab, and for a confirmed recipe save to clear only the numbers it carried; a sixth for the batch, tax and target fields to keep a value typed while an earlier save of the field was pending; a seventh for the desk to take the bar's own anchors, the tax notice else the grid's section, when the Equipment module is off; an eighth for a write to carry the revision the rows were rendered from rather than one refreshed since; a ninth for every stored entry to carry the revision that stored it, so a stale copy's edit or deletion of an entry changed since the tab read it is refused as a conflict, and for a late ledger answer never to roll a tab back; a tenth for the row memo to carry the peer count, so open Details follow a same-item offer coming or going below eight peers, and for "Use quotes" to clear only the typed prices; an eleventh for a forced sales refresh asked for while another item's read was in flight to run afterwards instead of being dropped; a twelfth for a listing to need its price and for the kept reads to go before the panel's fallback read, so one refresh makes one read; a thirteenth for the kept reads to run when a hidden tab becomes visible, and for the desk to label a failed sales refresh, the last good read shown as such; a fourteenth for a current tab's edit to be stored whatever the clocks say, and for the failed-read label to reach a cached sample too small for a scenario; a fifteenth for the equipment panel to mark a cached estimate after a failed refresh as the desk does, and for a full ledger to refuse new entries with a message rather than drop one silently). Resale and outcome memos turn over every five minutes so fills age out of the window. Observation of the player's own crafts is **not** implemented: no verified source (API or DOM) for own craft events was available offline, so manual entry ships first and the UI says "nothing is observed automatically".

## 7. Limitations and unverified live behaviour

- Everything above the pure models is verified against synthetic fixtures only: the live row text (stat/durability lines), the tax notice format, BUY detection via `textContent`, the Details button on real rows, and the Craft Desk's anchoring under the real equipment panel are on the manual checklist.
- Whether a fill's `money` is the buyer's total or the seller's proceeds is not verified; the tax is applied once to that figure.
- Recipes, craft outcome odds and any roll/stat distribution are not known offline; the desk never fills them in.
- The row reader's stat heuristic (first bare integer before the price) may not match the live page; it fails closed to `Stats unreadable`.
- The stat percentile among listed peers uses the rows on the page (12 per page before Load more), so it often reports `needs 8 listed peers`.
- Sales for an item are read when it is selected in the grid or picked on the desk; rows of other items keep the last twelve items' fills only.
- jsdom benchmark numbers are diagnostic; real Performance traces on the live page remain to be recorded.
- No live API, no real key, no real account was used.

## 10. Review round (second pass, same day)

A diff review of the four commits found 17 issues; all were fixed on the branch and covered by tests before the follow-up push. The three confirmed at runtime first:

- **Sales read loop.** The panel's item and the Craft Desk's item re-triggered each other's sales read on every scan (34 worker messages and 17 scans in 3 s on the fixture). Sales freshness is now kept per item (`salesByCode`), a failed read is not asked again for 15 s, and the test `reads the panel's item and the desk's item once each` pins it.
- **Panel re-insertion.** The equipment bar and the desk moved each other on every scan, blurring any focused desk field. A panel now tolerates the extension's own panels between it and its anchor; `keeps both panels in place and a focused desk field focused across native mutations` pins it. Age labels no longer count as changed markup, so a passing second does not rewrite a panel either.
- **False ledger success.** A worker that did not answer was reported as "Recorded". The desk now confirms a save only on the worker's stored list, keeps the form or the pending price for another try, and takes one ledger write at a time (buttons disabled while saving).

The rest: the price rank and the pace are computed over the same comparable fills as the median; focus returns to the picked matrix cell or ledger row, not the first one with the same action; clicks on the annotation fall through to the row again except on its button and its Details text; the 36 matrix cells and the outcome estimates are memoised per sales snapshot, prices, tax and recipe; typed values stop shadowing settings changed elsewhere; two identical listings keep separate Details; a list container is trusted only from two rows or more; ledger dates are local; a cell without a usable price is never painted green; a ledger basis is "manual" only when every used price was typed; the unused `craftInputs`, `FIXTURE` and bucket-table paths were removed and the previously unused `listingForProceeds` and `rankPlans` now drive the break-even listing line and the "best" caption; one non-negative number parser (`quality.nonNegative`) replaced four copies; the benchmark's quiet-window field is measured. Crafted pieces now compare with fills at 90–100% durability when the fills carry one. A second pass over the fix commits found eight more, fixed the same way: the desk's pace was measured over every fill instead of the comparable ones; the sales error and the read in flight were single slots shared by the panel's item and the desk's item (now per item); a file import could overlap a running save (the guard now sits in the save itself); a finishing save could close a form opened meanwhile; a typed setting was dropped before the save was confirmed (now kept, with a notice, until it is); a form without quantities read as "manual"; a single-row page kept an old list container and re-read styles every scan; and the desk had its own copy of the panel placement rule (now shared). Codex CLI was not available in the build environment (not installed, no network, no key), so both passes used the built-in reviewer. The repository's Codex GitHub reviewer then reviewed the first push when the PR was marked ready: of its three findings two were already fixed by the first pass (the sales loop, the rank sample) and the third, a stale tab replacing the shared ledger, is fixed by revisioned, merge-on-conflict writes with tombstones. Its fifth pass found that a tombstone compared by time let a stale edit stamped after the deletion revive the entry (tombstones now carry the revision of the deletion and the writer's base revision decides) and that a recipe save confirmed late cleared numbers typed meanwhile for another item (only the numbers the save carried, for the same item, are cleared). Its sixth pass found the same pattern on the batch, tax and target fields, where a value typed while an earlier save was pending was replaced by the confirmed one; the typed text is now cleared only while it is still the saved value. Its seventh pass found that with the Equipment module off and no tax notice on the page the desk had no anchor and was cleared although the grid was there; both panels now share one placement rule (`dom.marketAnchor`: the tax notice, else the grid's section), covered by a lifecycle test with the module off, a notice appearing later and a navigation round trip. Its eighth pass found a 100 ms window in which a refresh had replaced the tab's ledger with a revision carrying another tab's deletion while the rows on screen still came from the previous one, so a click on such a row wrote with the newer base and the worker took the edit as made in knowledge of the deletion; the desk now sends the revision its rows were rendered from, and the content script passes it through unchanged (a desk test and a lifecycle test that clicks inside that window). Its ninth pass found that a sale recorded in one tab was undone by the other tab's later move on its stale crafted copy (the newer timestamp won), and that a slow ledger answer arriving after the tab's own write rolled the desk back to the older revision; every stored entry now carries the revision of the write that stored it and the worker refuses, as a reported conflict, an edit or deletion from a tab that read an older revision, and a tab adopts a ledger answer only when its revision is above the one it holds (tests in the ledger, the worker, the desk and the lifecycle). Its tenth pass found that open Details kept a stale "(N now)" peer count while a same-item offer came or went below eight peers, since the row memo carried the rank alone (it now carries the count too), and that "Use quotes" reset every session input of the desk, discarding a recipe, a pending price or a pasted import being entered (it now clears the typed scrap and steel prices only). Its eleventh pass found that a forced sales refresh for one item, asked for while another item's read was in flight, was dropped: the single-flight guard returned before recording it and the follow-up read was not forced, so a fresh snapshot was kept. Reads asked for during another item's read are now kept with their force flag and run, one at a time, when that read finishes (a lifecycle test holds the panel's read while the desk's refresh is clicked). Its twelfth pass found that "List…" confirmed with an empty price stored a listed entry at no price (the list move now refuses it, the desk says a listing needs its price, and a stored "listed" entry without one reads as crafted), and that after a read the panel's non-forced fallback could start before the kept forced read of the same item, making two reads for one refresh (the kept reads now go first and the fallback runs only when nothing is being read or waiting). Its thirteenth pass found that reads kept while a tab was hidden were never run (they now run when the tab becomes visible) and that the desk kept presenting a cached snapshot as current after a failed refresh, unlike the panel (the cell is now marked, its title and the detail's lines say the last read failed and the last good read is shown; an item with no read says "read failed"). Its fourteenth pass found that an entry imported with a timestamp from the future could not be edited by a current tab, the worker keeping the newer stamp and the desk reporting success (the worker now stores a current writer's copy whatever the clocks say; a timestamp decides only which copy of an imported file is offered), and that the failed-read label did not reach the EV line and the listing guidance of an item whose cached sample was too small for a scenario (it now does). Its fifteenth pass found that the equipment panel hid a failed refresh whenever a cached snapshot existed, the row, its Details and the collapsed summary presenting the old median as current (they now carry a ⚠ mark and "last read failed" until a read succeeds), and that a new entry accepted into a full ledger could be cut by the 500-entry bound without a word when the stored entries sorted after it (a full ledger now takes no new entry and never drops a stored one; the worker reports the ids it could not hold, the desk keeps the form and says the ledger is full).

## 9. Expansion ideas (ranked by available evidence)

1. **Resale by stat band.** If `transaction.getPaginatedTransactions` items expose the piece's stat (the 1.4.1 fixtures show `item.skills` alongside `state`), keep it on each fill and price good rolls against plain rolls; the Craft Desk's outcome table then becomes an observed distribution instead of a single sales-weighted outcome. Evidence: the API probe of 2026-09-03 recorded the item object on fills; one probe confirms the field.
2. **Observed own crafts and sales for the ledger.** The same transaction feed filtered by the player's own id (documented `userId` filter in the openapi notes) would let the ledger mark listings and sales automatically, labelled `observed`, replacing manual entry for sales while keeping manual cost basis. Needs one keyed probe of the feed's transaction types.
3. **Case → craft → scrap loop.** The case models already value drops under a scrap/sell policy with audited odds (543,747 opens); adding "craft from the scraps" as a third policy branch on the case strip would show when opening cases for scraps to craft beats selling them, using the desk's EV. Evidence exists for the odds; it needs the real recipes first.
